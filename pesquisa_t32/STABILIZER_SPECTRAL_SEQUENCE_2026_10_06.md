# Stabilizer spectral sequence and the N -> N/2 recursion

**Research note — 2026-10-06**

## Purpose and scope

This note records a new conceptual route for the orbit-chain/homology part of the project. It is intentionally isolated from the submission manuscripts and existing verified proofs. Nothing here replaces the current cubic theorem, the audited folding lemmas, or the computational certificates.

The goal is to explain the experimentally observed long-orbit/short-orbit dichotomy and the apparent recursion from N to N/2 by a stabilizer spectral sequence.

## External source that motivated the construction

The OpenAI math repository manuscript *Bounded-degree coboundary expanders in every dimension* uses a group action on an acyclic simplicial complex and decomposes simplicial chain modules into permutation modules associated with simplex stabilizers. Its argument relies on stabilizers of odd order over F_2, so the corresponding permutation modules are projective and the additional modular homology disappears.

Our situation is complementary: G=C_N with N a power of two, coefficients F_2, and the nontrivial stabilizers are 2-groups. Thus the failure of projectivity is expected to be carried exactly by the short orbits.

This source is used as a technique/inspiration, not as authority for the statements below.

## 1. Candidate bicomplex

Let

    G = C_N,
    X = Delta^{N-1},

with G acting by cyclic permutation of the vertices. Index chain degree by support size:

    M_d = C_{d-1}(X; F_2).

For each G-orbit of d-element supports S, let

    H_S = Stab_G(S).

Then M_d is a permutation module and decomposes as

    M_d ~= direct_sum_[S] Ind_{H_S}^G F_2.

Choose a projective resolution P_* -> F_2 over F_2[G] and form

    K^{q,d} = Hom_{F_2[G]}(P_q, M_d).

Filtering in the resolution direction gives, by Shapiro,

    E_1^{d,q}
      ~= direct_sum_{[S], |S|=d} H^q(H_S; F_2).       (1)

The indexing convention M_d=C_{d-1} should be kept explicit to avoid an off-by-one error.

## 2. The q=0 row is the orbit-sum complex

For every stabilizer H_S,

    H^0(H_S;F_2)=F_2.

Hence

    E_1^{d,0} = H^0(G;M_d) = M_d^G.

A basis of M_d^G is given by sums over distinct G-orbits of supports. Therefore the q=0 row is exactly the invariant/orbit-sum chain complex already used in the project, with its boundary induced from deleting vertices.

This is stronger than an analogy: the existing orbit-sum complex occurs canonically as the q=0 row of (1).

## 3. Upper rows are supported only on short orbits

If S is a long orbit then H_S=1. Therefore

    H^q(H_S;F_2)=0  for q>0.

Consequently

    every E_1^{d,q}, q>0, is supported exclusively on short orbits.

This gives a conceptual interpretation of the existing long-vs-short orbit split:

- long orbits are the free/projective part of the action;
- short orbits carry the modular obstruction.

Related norm viewpoint: on a free group-algebra summand the norm from coinvariants to invariants is an isomorphism and sends the orbit class to the orbit sum. Short orbits are precisely where this free-module picture fails in characteristic two.

## 4. Face maps: restriction/corestriction, not restriction alone

A crucial correction is that, after applying Shapiro, a face map is not uniformly a restriction map.

For an incidence between orbit representatives, let H and K denote the source and target stabilizers and put L=H intersection K. The induced component has Mackey-type form

    H^q(H)
      --res--> H^q(L)
      --cor--> H^q(K),

with the appropriate sum over incidences/double cosets. Since G=C_N is abelian, conjugation terms disappear.

In degree zero,

    cor_L^K(1) = [K:L] * 1.

Thus the stabilizer multiplicities appearing in the existing folding/orbit-sum formulas should be recoverable as the degree-zero shadow of this restriction/corestriction formalism.

This is a target for a formal lemma; it should not be assumed without checking the exact incidence multiplicities.

## 5. Antipodal lemma for N=2^k

Assume N=2^k. Every nontrivial subgroup of C_N contains the unique order-two subgroup generated by translation by N/2.

Therefore, if S has nontrivial stabilizer, S is invariant under the antipodal involution

    x -> x + N/2,

so S is a union of antipodal pairs.

Delete one vertex x from S. Then S\{x} contains x+N/2 but not x, so it is not invariant under the antipodal involution. Since every nontrivial subgroup contains that involution,

    Stab(S\{x}) = 1.

Hence:

**Antipodal face lemma.**
Every codimension-one face of a short support is long.

## 6. Consequence for the first differential

On every upper row q>0, a d_1 component starting from a short orbit lands in a long orbit, whose positive-degree stabilizer cohomology is zero.

Therefore, with the vertical-first convention used in (1),

    d_1 = 0 on all rows q>0.                         (2)

This is a structural statement and sharply localizes where the observed N -> N/2 recursion can occur: it cannot be the upper-row d_1.

## 7. Why d_2 is the natural recursion candidate

Start with a short support S. To delete two vertices and end at another short support, the two deleted vertices must form an antipodal pair

    {x, x+N/2}.

Write the antipodal quotient as

    pi: Z_N -> Z_{N/2}.

A short support S descends to a support bar(S) in Z_{N/2}. Removing the antipodal pair above x is exactly

    bar(S) -> bar(S)\{bar(x)}.

Thus the combinatorial support of the first possible higher differential

    d_2: E_2^{d,q} -> E_2^{d-2,q-1}

is naturally identified with the one-vertex deletion incidence of the N/2 problem.

This provides a structural explanation for the experimentally observed recursion N -> N/2.

## 8. What is proved and what is not

### Structurally established / standard once conventions are fixed

1. The permutation-module decomposition of M_d.
2. The Shapiro E_1 description (1).
3. The identification of q=0 with the orbit-sum invariant complex.
4. Vanishing of long-orbit contributions for q>0.
5. For N=2^k, every nontrivial stabilizer contains the antipodal involution.
6. Every one-vertex face of a short support is long.
7. Consequently d_1=0 on q>0, subject to the stated spectral-sequence convention.

### Still to be proved carefully

1. The exact Mackey/Shapiro formula for every face component, including multiplicities.
2. Recovery of the previously audited folding coefficients from the q=0 restriction/corestriction formula.
3. The explicit chain-level calculation of d_2 using a 2-periodic resolution.
4. The coefficient attached to deletion of an antipodal pair.
5. The desired identification, if true,

       d_2(N) = boundary(N/2)

   after the correct degree shifts and stabilizer factors.
6. Convergence/abutment statements in the exact bounded bicomplex used here.
7. The induction needed to turn the separate computations H_3,H_5,H_7,H_9 into uniform odd-degree vanishing.

No claim of a general odd-degree vanishing theorem is made in this note.

## 9. Immediate research program

The next work should be concentrated in four lemmas.

### Lemma A — Shapiro/stabilizer page

Construct the bicomplex precisely and prove (1), including the indexing and convergence statement needed in the finite range.

### Lemma B — Mackey face formula

For each face incidence, derive the res/cor formula and prove that q=0 reproduces the existing orbit-sum/folding multiplicities.

### Lemma C — Antipodal d_1 vanishing

Package the argument of Sections 5-6 as a short formal lemma valid for every N=2^k.

### Lemma D — Recursive d_2

Choose an explicit 2-periodic resolution for C_{2^j}, compute d_2 at chain level, and determine whether deletion of an antipodal pair has exactly the coefficient of the boundary in the N/2 orbit complex.

Lemma D is now the principal mathematical bottleneck.

## 10. Strategic consequence

Until Lemma D is resolved, manually searching for isolated cocycles in H_7 or further odd degrees has lower expected value.

If Lemma D gives the expected identification, the separate observations

    H_3 = H_5 = H_7 = H_9 = 0

may become instances of one inductive mechanism driven by stabilizer cohomology and the recursion N -> N/2.

If the coefficient in Lemma D fails, that failure will still identify precisely where the empirical recursion breaks and will provide a much cleaner falsification target than ad hoc cocycle searches.
