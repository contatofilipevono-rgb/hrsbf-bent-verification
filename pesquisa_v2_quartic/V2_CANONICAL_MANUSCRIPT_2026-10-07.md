# V2 CANONICAL MANUSCRIPT — Exact linearity index of a quartic rotation-symmetric bent family

## Abstract

We study an explicit interleaved family of quartic rotation-symmetric bent Boolean functions F_r in n=8r variables, obtained from r disjoint copies of an eight-variable quartic rotation-symmetric bent seed. We determine both its ordinary and relaxed linearity indices exactly:

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=r=n/8

for every r>=1. Consequently the family lies outside the completed Maiorana--McFarland class M# with exact M-subspace deficiency 3n/8. The finite seed obstruction is proved algebraically through the fourth polarization of its quartic part: the induced map on Lambda^2(F_2^8) has a six-dimensional kernel containing no nonzero decomposable bivector. An elementary Pluecker-coordinate argument proves this nonintersection, while independent exact certificates provide reproducibility. The all-r theorem then follows from a direct block-projection argument and does not require exhaustive computation in growing dimension.

## 1. Setup

For a Boolean function g:F_2^m->F_2 write

    D_a g(x)=g(x+a)+g(x).

An M-subspace is a linear subspace U such that

    D_aD_b g = 0

for every a,b in U. The linearity index ind(g) is the maximum dimension of an M-subspace. A relaxed M-subspace requires D_aD_b g only to be constant, and r-ind(g) denotes the corresponding maximum dimension. Thus

    ind(g) <= r-ind(g).

Let f:F_2^8->F_2 be the quartic rotation-symmetric bent seed whose ANF is the XOR of the cyclic orbits generated by

    (0,1), (0,1,2,3), (0,1,2,5), (0,1,3,5).

For r>=1 let G_r be the ordinary direct sum of r copies of f on disjoint eight-variable blocks. Let F_r be obtained from G_r by the interleaving permutation

    B_j=(x_j,x_{j+r},...,x_{j+7r}), 0<=j<r.

Then n=8r. The interleaved presentation is rotation-symmetric, and F_r is linearly equivalent to G_r by a coordinate permutation.

## 2. Fourth polarization and the seed obstruction

Let Q be the homogeneous quartic part of f and put V=F_2^8. Define

    T(a,b,c,d)=D_aD_bD_cD_d Q.

Because Q has degree four, T is independent of the evaluation point and is multilinear. In characteristic two, repeated directions annihilate finite differences:

    D_aD_a h = 0.

Hence T vanishes whenever two arguments coincide and induces a well-defined map

    Phi: Lambda^2 V -> (Lambda^2 V)^*

through

    Phi(a wedge b)(c wedge d)=T(a,b,c,d).

Exact row reduction in the standard 28-dimensional wedge basis gives

    rank(Phi)=22,
    dim ker(Phi)=6.

A nonzero decomposable bivector a wedge b corresponds to an alternating matrix of rank 2. Therefore the key question is whether ker(Phi) meets the set of nonzero decomposable bivectors.

### Proposition 1

The kernel of Phi contains no nonzero decomposable bivector.

### Proof

Write the Pluecker coordinates of a bivector as

    p_ij=a_i b_j+a_j b_i, 0<=i<j<=7.

Row reduction of Phi parametrizes its kernel by six free coordinates

    u=p37, v=p46, w=p47, x=p56, y=p57, z=p67.

For a decomposable bivector, the Grassmann--Pluecker relations hold. Four of them reduce on this kernel to

    R1: v y + w x + z = 0,

    R2: u v + u w + u y + u z + w + x = 0,

    R3: u + v + v y + v z + w y + w z + x + y + z = 0,

    R4: u x + u z + v + v z + w z + x + x y + x z = 0.

From R1,

    z=v y+w x.

Substitution into the remaining equations reduces the problem to the four possible pairs (x,y) in F_2^2.

If (x,y)=(0,0), the equations imply v=0, then u=0, w=0, and z=0.

If (x,y)=(0,1), one obtains

    u w+u+w=0

and

    u+v w+w+1=0.

The first equation forces u=w=0, contradicting the second.

If (x,y)=(1,0), the reduced equations are

    u v+w+1=0,
    u+v w+v+1=0,
    u w+u+v w+v+1=0.

For v=0 they force w=u=1 and contradict the third equation. For v=1 the first two equations give respectively w=u+1 and w=u, again a contradiction.

If (x,y)=(1,1), two reduced equations give simultaneously

    u+w+1=0
    and
    u+w=0,

which is impossible.

Thus the only decomposable point in ker(Phi) is the zero bivector. QED.

### Corollary 2

    ind(f)=r-ind(f)=1.

### Proof

Suppose U is a relaxed M-subspace with dim U>=2 and choose independent a,b in U. Then D_aD_b f is constant. Applying two further derivatives gives

    T(a,b,c,d)=0

for every c,d, so a wedge b lies in ker(Phi). Since a,b are independent, a wedge b is a nonzero decomposable bivector, contradicting Proposition 1. Hence r-ind(f)<=1.

Conversely every nonzero one-dimensional subspace <a> is an M-subspace because D_aD_a f=0. Therefore

    1 <= ind(f) <= r-ind(f) <= 1,

and the result follows. QED.

### Reproducibility check

Independent exact computation gives the nonzero alternating-rank spectrum of ker(Phi):

    3 elements of rank 4,
    60 elements of rank 8,
    0 elements of rank 2.

This independently certifies the absence of nonzero decomposable bivectors. A separate exhaustive certificate checks all 32,385 independent direction pairs at all 256 inputs and again obtains r-ind(f)=1.

## 3. Exact index of the infinite family

### Theorem 3

For every r>=1,

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=r=n/8.

### Proof

It suffices first to work with the ordinary direct sum G_r.

Let U be a relaxed M-subspace of G_r. For a,b in U, write their block components as

    a=(a_0,...,a_{r-1}),
    b=(b_0,...,b_{r-1}).

Because the summands of G_r depend on disjoint variable blocks,

    D_aD_b G_r(x)
      = sum_i D_{a_i}D_{b_i}f(x^(i)).

The left-hand side is constant. Each summand depends only on its own block. Varying one block while fixing all others shows that every

    D_{a_i}D_{b_i}f

is itself constant. Therefore each block projection pi_i(U) is a relaxed M-subspace of f. By Corollary 2,

    dim pi_i(U)<=1.

The block-projection map

    U -> direct-sum_i pi_i(U)

is injective. Hence

    dim U <= sum_i dim pi_i(U) <= r,

so

    r-ind(G_r)<=r.

For the reverse inequality, choose one nonzero direction e_i in each block and let

    U_r=span(e_0,...,e_{r-1}).

For arbitrary

    a=sum_i alpha_i e_i,
    b=sum_i beta_i e_i,

the disjoint-block decomposition gives

    D_aD_bG_r
      = sum_i D_{alpha_i e_i}D_{beta_i e_i}f.

For each i, either alpha_i=0 or beta_i=0, so that term is zero, or alpha_i=beta_i=1, in which case

    D_{e_i}D_{e_i}f=0.

Thus D_aD_bG_r=0 for all a,b in U_r. Hence U_r is an M-subspace of dimension r and

    ind(G_r)>=r.

Combining

    r <= ind(G_r) <= r-ind(G_r) <= r

gives

    ind(G_r)=r-ind(G_r)=r.

Finally F_r is obtained from G_r by a coordinate permutation, which bijectively maps ordinary and relaxed M-subspaces to subspaces of the same type and dimension. Therefore

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=r=n/8.

QED.

### Corollary 4

Assuming the standard M# criterion that membership requires an M-subspace of dimension n/2,

    F_r notin M#

for every r>=1. Moreover,

    n/2-ind(F_r)=4r-r=3r=3n/8.

Thus the family has exact M-subspace deficiency 3n/8.

## 4. Structural interpretation

The exterior-square formulation gives a general sufficient criterion for quartic Boolean functions: if the kernel of the fourth-polarization map

    Phi_g: Lambda^2 V -> (Lambda^2 V)^*

contains no nonzero decomposable bivector, then r-ind(g)=1.

For the present seed, the kernel has dimension six. A further exact analysis of the cyclic rotation action gives the corrected module decomposition

    ker(Phi) ~= V_4 direct-sum V_2,

not V_6. This representation-theoretic refinement is not needed for Theorem 3 and is best regarded as structural supplementary information.

## 5. Position relative to prior work

The contribution is not the first existence result for rotation-symmetric bent functions outside M#. Recent work already establishes such families, including results concerning maximum-degree rotation-symmetric constructions and quartic families in other admissible dimensions.

The result established here is quantitative: for this explicit infinite quartic rotation-symmetric family in n=8r variables, both ordinary and relaxed linearity indices are determined exactly:

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=n/8.

Claims of priority should remain conservative unless a dedicated bibliographic comparison establishes more.

## 7. Audit record

Two exploratory errors were identified and removed before this canonical version:

1. an incorrect preliminary identification ker(Phi)~=V_6; the corrected module type is V_4 direct-sum V_2;
2. an incorrect appeal to bilinearity of finite differences in their direction arguments; the proof of Theorem 3 instead uses direct block decomposition.

Neither correction changes the final seed obstruction or the exact n/8 theorem.
