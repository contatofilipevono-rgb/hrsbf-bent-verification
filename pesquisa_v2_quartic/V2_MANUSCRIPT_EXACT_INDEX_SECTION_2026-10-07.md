## Exact linearity index of an interleaved quartic rotation-symmetric bent family

Let f:F_2^8 -> F_2 be the quartic rotation-symmetric bent function whose ANF is the XOR of the cyclic orbits generated by

    (0,1), (0,1,2,3), (0,1,2,5), (0,1,3,5).

For r>=1, let

    G_r = f direct-sum ... direct-sum f

be the ordinary r-fold direct sum on r disjoint blocks of eight variables. Let F_r be the Boolean function obtained from G_r by the interleaving coordinate permutation

    B_j=(x_j,x_{j+r},...,x_{j+7r}),  0<=j<r.

Thus F_r acts on n=8r variables. The interleaved presentation is rotation-symmetric, while F_r and G_r are linearly equivalent.

### Lemma 1 — The base block is ell-optimal

The eight-variable base block satisfies

    ind(f)=r-ind(f)=1.

#### Proof

For every unordered pair of distinct nonzero directions a,b in F_2^8, evaluate

    D_a D_b f(x)
      = f(x)+f(x+a)+f(x+b)+f(x+a+b)

at all x in F_2^8.

There are

    C(255,2)=32,385

such direction pairs. Exact exhaustive evaluation shows that no independent pair has constant second derivative.

A relaxed M-subspace of dimension at least two would contain independent a,b for which D_a D_b f is constant. Hence no such relaxed M-subspace exists and

    r-ind(f)<=1.

Every one-dimensional subspace is an ordinary M-subspace, since D_a D_a f=0. Therefore

    1<=ind(f)<=r-ind(f)<=1,

and consequently

    ind(f)=r-ind(f)=1.  □

In the terminology of Kudin et al. (2026), f is ell-optimal.

### Theorem — Exact n/8 law

For every r>=1,

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=r=n/8.

#### Proof

Because G_r is the direct sum of r copies of f, repeated application of the relaxed-linearity-index direct-sum inequality of Polujan--Pott gives

    r-ind(G_r)
       <= sum_{j=1}^r r-ind(f)
       = r.

Since ordinary M-subspaces are relaxed M-subspaces,

    ind(G_r)<=r-ind(G_r)<=r.                       (1)

For the reverse inequality, choose a nonzero direction e_j supported entirely in the j-th eight-variable block and put

    U_r=span(e_1,...,e_r).

If two directions are supported in different blocks, the corresponding mixed second derivative of the direct sum vanishes. Inside a given block, the restriction of U_r contains only 0 and e_j, and D_{e_j}D_{e_j}f=0. Hence

    D_aD_b G_r=0

for all a,b in U_r. Thus U_r is an ordinary M-subspace of dimension r and

    ind(G_r)>=r.                                  (2)

Combining (1) and (2),

    ind(G_r)=r-ind(G_r)=r.

Finally, F_r is obtained from G_r by an invertible coordinate permutation. Both ordinary and relaxed linearity index are invariant under nonsingular affine equivalence. Hence

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=r=n/8.  □

### Corollary — Exact deficiency from the M# threshold

A bent function on n variables belongs to the completed Maiorana--McFarland class M# only if it possesses an M-subspace of dimension n/2. For F_r,

    n/2 - ind(F_r)
       = 4r-r
       = 3r
       = 3n/8.

Therefore

    F_r notin M#

for every r>=1, with exact M-subspace deficiency 3n/8.  □

## Position relative to prior work

The contribution is NOT the first existence result for rotation-symmetric bent functions outside M#. Polujan--Kudin--Pasalic (IEEE TIT 2026) explicitly solve that existence problem, prove Su's maximum-degree RS family outside M# for all n>=8, and show the quartic Carlet--Gao--Liu family outside M# for infinitely many dimensions.

The present theorem is narrower and quantitative: it determines the exact ordinary and relaxed linearity indices of the explicit quartic RS family F_r for every n=8r.

### Carlet--Gao--Liu quartic RS family

Their principal quartic family requires n=2m with m odd, hence n=2 mod 4. Our family has n=8r. The admissible dimension sets are disjoint.

### Su maximum-degree RS family

Su's family has degree n/2. Our family has degree 4. Thus for n=8r>=16 the families cannot be EA-equivalent by algebraic degree.

At n=8 both have degree four and both have linearity index one. The exact second-derivative spectrum certificate stored in this repository separates F1 from Su_8.

### Pasalic et al. 2024 explicit eight-variable examples

The exact certificate stored in this repository shows that Examples 28, 29 and 37 each admit an independent pair a,b with D_aD_b f=0, and therefore have ordinary linearity index at least two. Since ind(F1)=1, all three are inequivalent to F1.

### Low and prescribed linearity index

Recent work on M-subspaces, concatenation constructions, and ell-optimal bent functions shows that small or controllable linearity index is itself prior art. Accordingly, novelty should not be claimed for the general concept of low index.

The specific statement established here is the exact identity

    ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=n/8

inside an infinite quartic rotation-symmetric bent family.

## Priority statement suitable for submission

A conservative formulation is:

> We determine the exact linearity index of an explicit infinite family of quartic rotation-symmetric bent functions in n=8r variables, proving ind(F_r)=r-ind(F_r)=n/8 for every r>=1. Recent work establishes rotation-symmetric bent functions outside the completed Maiorana--McFarland class and develops constructions with small or controllable linearity index. In the literature examined in our priority audit, however, we did not locate this exact n/8 law for a previously published infinite quartic rotation-symmetric family.

Do not replace the last sentence by "the first known" without a stronger bibliographic basis.

The following repository artifacts provide the exact computational layer:

1. `certify_base_relaxed_index.py`
   - reconstructs f from the four orbit seeds;
   - checks all 32,385 unordered independent direction pairs;
   - evaluates all 256 points for each pair;
   - certifies r-ind(f)=ind(f)=1.

2. `certify_pasalic_2024_inequivalence.py`
   - reconstructs Examples 28, 29 and 37;
   - verifies bentness;
   - gives exact M-subspace witnesses separating them from F1.

3. `certify_su8_inequivalence.py`
   - reconstructs F1 and Su_8;
   - computes the complete second-derivative (degree,weight) multiset;
   - certifies EA inequivalence.

The all-r theorem itself is not computational: once the finite base certificate r-ind(f)=1 is established, the upper bound follows from the published direct-sum theorem and the lower bound from the explicit M-subspace U_r.
