# Universal antipodal necessity for RS bent functions of degree at most 3

## Status

Algebraic corollary of the odd-order fixed-space reduction plus the power-of-two Antipodal Rule. Independently checked by exhaustive enumeration in dimensions 6 and 10 on 2026-10-06.

## Theorem

Let n be a positive even integer and let f:F_2^n -> F_2 be rotation-symmetric, bent, and of algebraic degree at most 3. Then the ANF of f contains the antipodal quadratic orbit

P_n(x) = sum_{i=0}^{n/2-1} x_i x_{i+n/2}.

Equivalently, the coefficient of the unique quadratic short orbit of length n/2 is 1.

For n=2 this is immediate, since every bent function has degree 2 and the only quadratic monomial is x_0 x_1.

## Proof

Write n=t m with t=2^s and m odd. For n>2, let T=sigma^t. Its order is m and U=Fix(T) has dimension t. Identify U with F_2^t by x_i=y_{i mod t}.

By the odd-order fixed-space restriction theorem for degree at most 3, g=f|_U is bent and rotation-symmetric, with degree at most 3.

If t>=4, the power-of-two Antipodal Rule implies that g contains

P_t(y)=sum_{j=0}^{t/2-1} y_j y_{j+t/2}.

If t=2, g is a bent Boolean function in two variables, hence its ANF contains y_0 y_1=P_2.

It remains to determine which orbit terms of f can contribute P_t under folding.

1. Linear orbits cannot contribute a quadratic term.

2. A full quadratic orbit generated by x_0 x_d folds to the sum of the t cyclic translates of y_0 y_{d mod t}, repeated m times. Since m is odd, only the t-translate sum matters. If d mod t != t/2, this is a non-antipodal quadratic orbit (or degenerates to a linear term when d mod t=0). If d mod t=t/2, each antipodal edge occurs twice among the t translates, hence the contribution is zero in F_2.

3. The unique short quadratic orbit is P_n, generated by x_0 x_{n/2}. Under folding, n/2=((m-1)/2)t+t/2, so it maps to antipodal pairs in t variables. Each target antipodal edge occurs exactly m times. Since m is odd, the folded contribution is exactly P_t.

4. A full cubic orbit folds to the t cyclic translates of a reduced monomial of degree 1, 2, or 3, again with odd multiplicity m. If the reduced degree is 2 and its separation is t/2, the same double-counting as in (2) makes the antipodal contribution zero. Otherwise it cannot contribute P_t.

5. A short cubic orbit can only have stabilizer of order 3; its support is {a,a+n/3,a+2n/3}. Such an orbit exists only when 3|m and folds to the total linear form, not to P_t.

Therefore the coefficient of P_t in g is exactly the coefficient of P_n in f. Since g is bent and necessarily contains P_t, f necessarily contains P_n. QED.

## Consequence for homogeneous cubics

A homogeneous cubic f has no quadratic terms, so the coefficient of P_n is zero. The theorem therefore immediately excludes homogeneous cubic RS bent functions in every positive even dimension.

This yields a compressed proof of the global cubic theorem once the odd-order fixed-space theorem and the power-of-two Antipodal Rule have been established.

## Exact computational controls

### n=6

All RS Boolean functions of degree <=3 were enumerated exactly.

- orbit generators (degrees 0 through 3): 9
- total candidates: 2^9 = 512
- bent functions: 48
- bent functions without P_6: 0

### n=10

All RS Boolean functions of degree <=3 were enumerated exactly.

- orbit generators (degrees 0 through 3): 19
- total candidates: 2^19 = 524288
- bent functions: 6336
- candidates without P_10: 2^18 = 262144
- bent functions without P_10: 0

The computations are validation only; they are not premises of the theorem.

## Literature positioning

Cusick and Sanger, *Rotation Symmetric Bent Boolean Functions for n=2p* (2017), prove the antipodal quadratic necessity for quadratic RS bent functions and related short-cycle results. The theorem above should therefore be presented as a degree-at-most-3 extension obtained from the fixed-space/folding architecture, not as the first observation of antipodal necessity.

A full priority claim requires a dedicated literature search.
